Container Security Best Practices for Developers on OpenShift

The final minutes. One table that takes the whole workshop back to the question the organization actually has: can we show, per DORA, that our containers are built and run securely? Every row is a practice from a previous step and the article it supports. Print it, or pin it in the team channel.

Prerequisites

  • Steps 1-6 walked through (the checklist is the summary of them)

The Digital Operational Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) has applied since 17 January 2025. This table is a developer-side working mapping built from the articles of the Regulation itself, not legal advice and not a compliance assessment. The detailed technical requirements sit in the regulatory technical standards that accompany the Act. If your organization has no compliance function to confirm the mapping, check each row against the Regulation text and the supervisory authority’s guidance (see References) before citing it to an auditor.

Two usage notes:

  • The checklist is developer-side. The Act also covers governance, incident classification and reporting, resilience testing programs and third-party oversight, which live with your compliance, risk and security teams.

  • Keep the table as the working artifact, not the legal one.

Next Steps

  • Give each team the Step 2 Containerfile as the default template for new services, and pin base images by digest.

  • Add the Step 4 gate to one pipeline as a report-only (non-failing) job for two weeks, then turn on enforcement.

  • Ask the platform team which NetworkPolicy and vault integration already exist for your projects (Steps 5 and 6) before building your own.

  • Ask about image signature verification on the registry: the natural follow-up to "only images we built may run".
