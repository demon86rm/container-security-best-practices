= Step 7: DORA Readiness Checklist
include::_attributes.adoc[]

The final minutes. One table that takes the whole workshop back to the question
the organization actually has: *can we show, per DORA, that our containers are
built and run securely?* Every row is a practice from a previous step and the
article it supports. Print it, or pin it in the team channel.

== Prerequisites

* Steps 1-6 walked through (the checklist is the summary of them)

IMPORTANT: The Digital Operational Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554)
has applied since 17 January 2025. This table is a *developer-side working
mapping* built from the articles of the Regulation itself, not legal advice and
not a compliance assessment. The detailed technical requirements sit in the
regulatory technical standards that accompany the Act. If your organization has
no compliance function to confirm the mapping, check each row against the
Regulation text and the supervisory authority's guidance (see References)
before citing it to an auditor.

[#checklist]
== The Checklist

[cols="4,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Practice | Where you did it | DORA article (working mapping)

| App is stateless, config in environment, port-bound, logs to stdout as JSON
| Steps 1, 2 (`01-12factor-platform/main.go`)
| Art. 10 (detection and logging)

| Base images are Red Hat UBI (or Hardened Images), versioned, ideally pinned by digest
| Step 2 (`02-secure-images/Containerfile.secure`)
| Art. 28 (third-party ICT risk)

| Multi-stage build: no toolchain, no source in the runtime image
| Step 2
| Art. 9 (protection and prevention)

| Container runs as non-root and is SCC-ready (`USER 1001`, group `0` ownership)
| Steps 2, 3
| Art. 9

| Root and privileged manifests are refused by admission; nobody holds a standing exemption
| Step 3 (SCC demo)
| Art. 9

| Identities are least-privilege; no standing `pods/exec` or `get secrets` for app code or developers
| Step 3 (RBAC demo)
| Art. 9

| Two or more replicas, probes, PodDisruptionBudget, graceful shutdown
| Steps 1, 3
| Art. 11 (response and recovery)

| Images are scanned for CVEs in the pipeline before push or promotion
| Step 4
| Art. 9 (vulnerability management), Art. 25 (testing)

| SBOM (SPDX) emitted per image as a CI artifact
| Step 4
| Art. 8 (identification and inventory), Art. 28

| Enforced build- and deploy-time policy gates fail the pipeline
| Step 4
| Art. 9

| No secrets in images or Git; secrets referenced from a vault and rotated without redeploy
| Step 5
| Art. 9

| The vault and operator are known third-party dependencies with an exit path
| Step 5
| Art. 28 (and Art. 30 for contracts, owned by your compliance team)

| Default-deny network policies; internal applications have no Route and accept only named callers
| Step 6
| Art. 9

| Production behavior is observed through collected logs, not shell access
| Steps 1, 3
| Art. 10, Art. 17 (incident management)
|===

Two usage notes:

* The checklist is *developer-side*. The Act also covers governance, incident
  classification and reporting, resilience testing programs and third-party
  oversight, which live with your compliance, risk and security teams.
* Keep the table as the working artifact, not the legal one.

== Next Steps

* Give each team the Step 2 Containerfile as the default template for new
  services, and pin base images by digest.
* Add the Step 4 gate to one pipeline as a report-only (non-failing) job for
  two weeks, then turn on enforcement.
* Ask the platform team which NetworkPolicy and vault integration already exist
  for your projects (Steps 5 and 6) before building your own.
* Ask about image signature verification on the registry: the natural follow-up
  to "only images we built may run".
